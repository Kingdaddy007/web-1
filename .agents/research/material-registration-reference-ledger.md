# Reference Intelligence — Material Registration

## Analysis contract

Decision informed: how the approved logo arrival should hand into a film-led hero without another generic wipe, technical showreel, or wallpaper-video layout.

Research questions:

1. How can the logo stage leave without reading as a wipe, curtain, door, or fade?
2. How can a tactile surface feel architectural rather than technological?
3. How can film and large editorial typography coexist without either becoming secondary decoration?
4. How can one strong opening return quickly to credible room and process proof?

All local Design Audit reports are mediated reference notes. `REPORTED` describes what a report says; it does not prove the original site's current behavior, technology, accessibility, or mobile support.

## Delegated full-corpus pass

A Luna Max Design Director worker reviewed the library index and all 30 numbered reports without modifying files. It confirmed complete sequence coverage and ranked evidence usefulness against the four research questions. Its top eight were Site 13 StringTune, Site 19 NomadaToast, Site 28 CALMM, Site 14 Terminal, Site 11 Poly, Site 29 Lounge Coffee, Site 21 Tidescape as the negative control, and Site 09 Joby. ## Cross-reference synthesis

Recurring useful logic:

- continuity is stronger when one physical idea governs the whole opening;
- a signature arrival should hand control back to readable proof;
- depth is more convincing when assets share camera, lighting, and perspective;
- narrative, ambient, and interactive motion must have separate ownership;
- spectacle must retire after it completes its communication job.

Negative patterns for TTA:

- black-space prestige as a substitute for room quality;
- particle shatter, liquid curtains, portal openings, glass cards, floating UI, and generic split masks;
- continuous pointer parallax before the room and copy are stable;
- multiple heavy systems in the first viewport;
- importing a reference's whole architecture because its production is polished.

## Translation ledger

### 1. Existing Constructed Axis arrival

- Decision: `KEEP`.
- Job: establish identity and disciplined authorship.
- Change: none through exact-master settlement.
- Rejection condition: any integration that distorts, recolors, crops, or restages the accepted geometry without a separate approval.

### 2. Single-material continuity

- Decision: `ADAPT` from Site 12.
- Job: make the opening background and hero feel like one world.
- Retained principle: one material persists across the transition.
- Changed form: no particles or continuous morph. The opening macro surface is derived from the actual hero-room wall panel and resolves into that same surface in context.
- Assets: matched macro material plate plus final room plate.
- Mobile/reduced motion: material still followed by room still.
- Cost/risk: believable texture and exact registration are required; reject if the surface looks unrelated or artificially generated.

### 3. Object-authored reveal and utility handoff

- Decision: `ADAPT` from Site 13.
- Job: let the logo leave through spatial consequence rather than an added transition.
- Retained principle: a real object or boundary causes the reveal and then retires.
- Changed form: the D-axis aligns to a wall-panel join; the TV/foreground plane occludes the logo during the pullback. No sword, stencil trail, kinetic impact, or cyber palette.
- Assets: room depth layers or a stable start/end media pair.
- Mobile/reduced motion: direct still-to-still change; no simulated camera depth.
- Cost/risk: occlusion must remain geometrically credible; reject if the logo merely fades behind a mask.

### 4. Film and typography separation

- Decision: `ADAPT` from Site 14 and Site 02.
- Job: keep the room cinematic without making it wallpaper behind interface copy.
- Retained principle: movement establishes the environment; typography enters only after the room settles into a stable reading interval.
- Changed form: no scroll-scrub frame sequence, fog, or permanent moving projection. The hero film plays one restrained movement and resolves to a still hold before the main copy completes its entrance.
- Assets: one room film, exact final poster, and a protected text-safe zone.
- Mobile/reduced motion: use the final poster with the same text in ordinary document flow.
- Rejection condition: copy covers architectural evidence, the film continues demanding attention while reading, or mobile requires a miniature desktop choreography.

### 5. Hook-to-proof pacing

- Decision: `ADAPT` from Site 19.
- Job: convert surprise into trust before the concept feels like a showreel.
- Retained principle: a high-attention opening is followed by quieter proof.
- Changed form: no shock or rupture. The room settles, copy becomes readable, and the first scroll exposes early-stage decisions.
- Assets: finished-room hero, site proof, concise evidence-led copy.
- Mobile/reduced motion: same chapter order without high-intensity motion.
- Rejection condition: a second dominant effect appears before the first proof beat.

### 6. Governing physics sentence

- Decision: `KEEP` as a planning rule, adapted from Site 23.
- Job: prevent mechanic collage.
- Rule: **Nothing wipes; spatial depth is restored.**
- Motion ownership: logo assembly establishes order; camera/depth registration reveals context; ambient hero motion begins only after the transition retires.
- Rejection condition: any effect moves for a different metaphor—liquid, arc, hinge, curtain, particle, or portal—in the same opening.

### 7. Coherent depth sandwich

- Decision: `ADAPT` from Site 28.
- Job: create credible foreground occlusion and a cinematic room reveal.
- Retained principle: scene assets share perspective and lighting while semantic UI remains DOM.
- Changed form: only genuine room layers—wall, television/console, sofa/table foreground, curtains. No floating cards, foliage, product island, or spring motion.
- Mobile: use a composed poster, not a miniature 3D stack.
- Reduced motion: final poster with ordinary crossfade.
- Cost/risk: requires clean masks or a stable depth map; reject if edges halo, geometry swims, or source resolution breaks.

### 8. Canvas UI or realtime depth

- Decision: `DEFER` to one isolated comparison slice.
- Visual job: preserve depth and occlusion during the material-to-room pullback.
- Simpler routes considered: still loses the transition; generated media risks geometric drift; DOM/CSS layered planes may already be sufficient.
- Canvas qualification: only if a contained depth-displacement or occlusion treatment is materially more convincing than the DOM baseline.
- Boundary: semantic logo layers, copy, navigation, and actions remain DOM. No Canvas UI import or dependency is authorized by this document.
- Fallback: DOM layered pullback and final static poster.
- Rejection condition: visual gain is marginal, mobile meaning is weaker, or cleanup/performance cannot be demonstrated.

## Approved handoff hypothesis

The reference corpus supports one governing idea, not a collage: **Material Registration**. A macro surface from the actual room carries the logo; spatial depth returns; real foreground geometry hides the mark; the final room settles; proof follows. Nothing else from the references is copied.

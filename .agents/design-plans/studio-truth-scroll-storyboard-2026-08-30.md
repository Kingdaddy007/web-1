## Portfolio window mechanics

- Five accepted project images remain in one centered `90vw × 82svh` gallery frame.
- Each incoming project reveals bottom-to-top through a vertical aperture; the image counter-translates by less than two percent.
- Panel order is explicit for all five frames, and a covered panel is hidden only after the incoming aperture reaches full coverage.
- Reverse scroll closes the same aperture. No panel travels laterally and no right-edge underlay remains visible.
- A low-opacity light seam softens the moving threshold; it does not wash or darken the source photography.
- Mobile and reduced motion use the static editorial sequence with no pinning or clipping.

## Desktop service mechanics

- Section height: approximately `420svh`; sticky stage: `100svh`.
- Progress index: `round(progress × 4)` for semantic active state; visual interpolation remains continuous.
- List occupies the left two-thirds; concise description sits in a stable right/lower reading zone.
- Names never cross the viewport edge and never overlap the description.
- The registration line is the only persistent graphic device.
- Entry and exit each receive at least 12% of the chapter range.
- No wheel/touch hijacking, snapping, or focus interception.

## Timing safeguards

- Service eyebrow and headline must be fully readable before the first service becomes active.
- Each description reaches full opacity by the middle third of its service interval.
- The final service holds at full readability before the inquiry begins.
- Fast scrolling must never leave all service entries invisible.

## Verification checkpoints

- Cold scroll from the practice film into services at desktop width.
- Reverse scroll from inquiry back through all five services.
- Fast wheel pass and trackpad-like small increments.
- 390×844 mobile normal flow.
- Native `prefers-reduced-motion` and explicit `?reduced=1`.
- Keyboard tab order reaches inquiry without entering decorative service layers.
- No horizontal overflow, clipped descenders, or low-contrast inactive text becoming the only readable state.

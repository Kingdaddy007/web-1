# Practice Film Plan

## Comments answered

## Current failure

The current practice section uses two portrait videos as adjacent media objects. That solves the source aspect ratio mechanically, but it changes the intended idea. The user’s suggestion was a full-screen practice environment whose background itself could be alive—not a pair of video cards.

## Communication job

Show that TTA remains close to the work: present at the site, attentive to decisions, and involved until the room resolves. This is the human-authority chapter, not another portfolio gallery.

## Recommended direction — One Full-Bleed Practice Film

Build one 6–10 second, seamless, 16:9 practice film that owns the entire chapter.

The frame should show:

- a real interior site with enough architecture to read at desktop width;
- one credible moment of discussion, inspection, or placement;
- restrained human movement rather than performance for camera;
- protected negative space for the title and note;
- warm but honest grade, retaining construction texture.

The chapter copy sits over the film in semantic DOM. It is not burned into the video.

### Source strategy

Use the strongest frame from the Akoka process material—preferably the site-conversation frame or a video frame where people and ceiling/room structure are both readable.

Production path:

1. Select the exact source frame and document why it carries the practice claim.
2. Restore and outpaint it to 16:9 while preserving faces, body positions, ceiling structure, wall openings, and spatial relationships.
3. Generate a restrained image-to-video move from that approved still.
4. Compare the first and last frames to prevent architectural drift.
5. Produce a poster frame from the same source for reduced motion and slow connections.

Do not generate new people, change gestures, invent equipment, or beautify the site until it becomes implausible.

### Camera direction

Recommended movement: a slow 3–5% push with a slight lateral drift that reveals more of the ceiling decision or conversation relationship. People may make only natural micro-movements. No sweeping orbit, dramatic rack focus, or rapid parallax.

The film should feel observed, not staged.

## Typography choreography

The practice heading can share the approved main-heading typeface, but it needs its own entrance:

1. The film establishes for approximately 400–700ms.
2. The eyebrow appears as a measured site notation.
3. The heading reveals by a vertical architectural datum—line by line, with the clip boundary aligned to a real wall or ceiling edge.
4. The note enters only after the heading has settled.

This is different from the material exposure in the hero-to-process threshold and from the formation behavior in the portfolio.

Provisional copy:

- eyebrow: `THE PRACTICE`
- heading: `The eye stays close to the work.`
- note: `From early site decisions to the final placement of light, furniture and art, attention stays present at every scale.`

The note must be reviewed against actual service claims before publication.

## Option B — Cinematic Still with 2.5D Depth

If a generated film cannot preserve the people and architecture, use one approved 16:9 still. Separate only broad depth zones—foreground, people, room, and ceiling—and apply extremely small camera movement. Add moving light/shadow only where physically believable.

This is safer than a visibly unstable AI video and still satisfies the desire for an alive full-screen environment.

## Use of the lived-room/gaming footage

Do not place it beside the practice footage. It can become a short lived-use insert in the portfolio chapter, where it demonstrates that the completed room supports ordinary life. Its copy must concern use or comfort, not site practice.

## Desktop choreography

- Enter from portfolio through a brief reduction in visual density.
- Let the film acquire the whole viewport before the title appears.
- Use slow film playback and minimal scroll-linked camera scale; the film should not scrub frame-for-frame with scroll.
- Release the section after the note and human action have both been readable.

## Mobile translation

- Use the original portrait process footage when it preserves the key people and action better than a cropped 16:9 render.
- Place copy in a protected upper/lower zone with an adaptive scrim derived from the frame luminance.
- Do not place two videos side by side or stack two autoplaying films.

## Reduced motion and performance

- Use the approved poster frame.
- Pause video when fully offscreen.
- Ship a mobile rendition rather than forcing the desktop file.
- Keep text readable before video metadata or autoplay resolves.

## Acceptance tests

- The section reads as one cinematic place, not two cards.
- The practice claim is supported by visible site behavior.
- Architecture and people remain stable through the generated move.
- The heading has a distinct, authored entrance.
- The gaming clip is not used as generic decoration.
- The poster frame preserves the complete communication job.

# CREATIVE BRIEF: TTA Designs

This brief is provisional. It is grounded in the direct screen recording, the 125 supplied keyframes, visible captions, the full user-supplied timestamped transcript, and the user's confirmed outreach constraints. The artifact is a private cinematic concept intended to earn TTA Designs' attention before direct discovery, not a complete or publishable website. The transcript is useful for the founder's argument, but garbled sentences and speaker turns must be founder-approved before publication.

## Prototype mandate

**Purpose:** Demonstrate what TTA's work could feel like in an authored digital experience and create enough interest for a proper brand, content, and website conversation.

**Immediate audience:** TTA's founder or decision-maker receiving the outreach.

**Simulated audience:** A design-conscious Lagos homeowner or property decision-maker evaluating TTA's taste, judgment, and process.

**Scope:** A compact residential-led cinematic slice with only the chapters that available evidence can support. It may show an arrival, a curated project or room sequence, one process or founder proof moment, and a polished inquiry preview without pretending to be a complete homepage.

**Truth boundary:** Omit missing services, team information, testimonials, project metadata, guarantees, and operational claims. Generated or transformed media must preserve the identity of the source project and remain private until rights are resolved.

## Strategic response

**Visitors should feel:** Calm, understood, reassured, and quietly impressed by the intelligence behind the finished room.

**Visitors should understand:** TTA Designs creates refined Lagos interiors by starting with how people live or work, how a space needs to function, and which decisions must happen early—not by decorating after the fact. Residential work leads this prototype; commercial work remains a secondary visible capability.

**Visitors should believe:** TTA can translate a lived brief into a refined, functional space; make the right decisions before expensive corrections; and guide the project through real-world uncertainty without making the client carry the whole process.

**Visitors should do:** Recognize that a considered inquiry path exists. The prototype may demonstrate a calm consultation interface, but it will not submit data or route to a live WhatsApp funnel before TTA approves the real experience.

**The studio must be known for first:** Making beautiful spaces work better for the people who use them, through a process that makes consequential decisions visible and manageable.

## Controlling problem

TTA's strongest proof is scattered across Instagram: finished rooms, founder education, site conditions, project captions, process warnings, and direct WhatsApp prompts. The prototype must compress that proof into one memorable trust argument without pretending to solve the complete website, flattening the studio into a generic portfolio, or overstating the available evidence.

## Working story spine (not approved concept)

1. **The space starts earlier than the finish.** Show the carcass-stage window where layout, electrical points, plumbing, and ceiling can still be made right.
2. **The decisions begin with people.** Show who uses the room, what needs to move, what needs to disappear, and what the room is being asked to support.
3. **The hidden work protects the visible result.** Make supervision, coordination, communication, artisans, and evolving site conditions part of the proof.
4. **The result is felt in daily life.** Move from the technical chain into warm, calm, lived-in rooms rather than presenting “luxury” as a decorative label.
5. **The invitation is a conversation.** Demonstrate a quiet, polished inquiry moment without activating a live WhatsApp or form workflow. The real route is a post-interest decision.

This sequence is a content hypothesis, not an approved page architecture or motion plan.

## Voice cues from the source

- Start with a practical question or a misconception, then answer it plainly.
- Use concrete consequences: flow, storage, comfort, coordination, corrections, delays, and cost.
- Be calm and direct, with a reassuring but not overpromising tone.
- Treat refinement as the consequence of thoughtful decisions, not as a status performance.
- Keep the founder's voice human and instructional; do not turn it into generic luxury copy.
- The supplied transcript's central language is usable as meaning, not as final quotation. Founder approval is required for exact wording.

## Visual direction inputs from the source

- Keep the existing black-and-white mark as an anchor; do not replace it with an invented luxury identity.
- Build around warm neutrals, cream, pale timber, black, white/grey stone, amber light, and one controlled accent rather than a fixed beige palette.
- Let room imagery carry comfort, texture, warmth, and lived use; let site imagery carry decision credibility.
- Use editorial explanation sparingly: a numbered principle, a concise reason, a visible consequence. The social card system is a reference, not a template to reproduce literally.
- If motion is later approved, it should reveal a decision or a transformation in meaning. Still images, captions, and reduced-motion equivalents must carry the same argument.

## Audience and proof burden

**Immediate outreach audience:** TTA's founder or decision-maker, who should feel that the concept recognizes the intelligence already present in the studio's work rather than imposing a generic luxury identity.

**Simulated primary visitor:** A Lagos homeowner or property decision-maker planning a substantial residential interior project, especially someone who values refinement but is anxious about costly mistakes, coordination, or loss of control.

**Secondary visitor:** A commercial or office decision-maker who needs a functional environment that supports movement, collaboration, and daily work. The profile explicitly includes Residential & Commercial, but this audience will not control the speculative prototype.

**Starting belief / anxiety:** I want a beautiful result, but I do not want a designer who only cares about appearance or a process that becomes stressful and opaque.

**Required evidence:**

- A completed space shown as a response to real people and real use.
- Visible decision logic: function, movement, storage, restraint, and technical timing.
- Process proof from early-stage site conditions through finished atmosphere.
- Founder or team authority demonstrated in the work, not only in a biography.
- A credible early-stage decision window: layout, electrical points, plumbing, and ceiling considered before finishes.
- A plain-language explanation of how supervision, communication, coordination, and structure reduce avoidable stress and cost.
- Clear but quiet inquiry route and honest expectations.

**Wrong-fit filter:** Visitors seeking only quick decoration, trend copying, isolated furniture purchases, or an image-only makeover should not be the controlling audience.

## Constraints

- **Content and assets:** Current evidence is a screen recording, 125 supplied keyframes, public Instagram material, and the full timestamped transcript. Original high-resolution assets are not yet available.
- **Prototype scope:** This is a partial outreach experience, not a complete site. Missing sections are preferable to invented information.
- **Brand / legal:** Image, video, client, and project rights are unknown. Any speculative concept using public work should remain private until permission is obtained.
- **Generated media:** Image-to-video treatment is optional and must follow a selected concept, use the strongest available source image, preserve room geometry and material identity, and avoid presenting invented spaces as TTA projects.
- **Accessibility:** Future visual sequences must preserve readable captions, keyboard/touch access, reduced-motion meaning, and non-video fallbacks.
- **Performance:** Do not assume a video-heavy homepage. Image-native proof needs intentional crops and static fallbacks before motion is approved.
- **Responsive:** The desktop recording does not establish mobile behavior. The concept must preserve the argument on narrow screens without copying desktop choreography.
- **Maintenance:** The current public presence is Instagram-led. The concept should be maintainable by a small studio without requiring a complex publishing system.
- **Timeline / resources:** Unknown. Start with the smallest convincing showroom slice around one project or one design principle.

## Anti-goals

- Do not make the site a generic hero/services/portfolio/testimonials/contact stack.
- Do not reduce TTA to luxury, modern, or minimal without evidence.
- Do not use beige, serif typography, stock rooms, and generic fade-ups as the concept.
- Do not treat rooms as interchangeable cards or dump the Instagram grid onto a webpage.
- Do not claim a market-exclusive moat before comparing this diagnosis with competitor evidence and verified delivery capability.
- Do not publish auto-captioned transcript wording as if it were approved founder copy.
- Do not use public images in a published or externally shared deliverable without permission.
- Do not introduce cinematic motion until it has a semantic job and a static equivalent.

## Approved opening-study method

The user evaluates visual direction more reliably from tangible compositions than from an abstract questionnaire. Exploration therefore follows `show -> react -> identify why -> refine -> test again`.

The first comparison is limited to three treatments built from the same strategy and source assets:

1. **Study A — Logo as an Aperture:** the line passing through the `D` becomes a controlled threshold into one dominant room. The logo acts as a gate into the practice.
2. **Study B — Logo as a Living Surface:** project motion briefly occupies the wordmark before escaping into the hero composition. The logo acts as a container becoming experience.
3. **Study C — Logo as a Curatorial Axis:** founder, process, room, and detail planes align around a restrained identity. The logo acts as the fixed intelligence organising the work.

The emotional sequence under test is `recognition -> curiosity -> authority -> desire`. These are opening treatments, not three unrelated websites or approved page architectures. The strongest treatment should be prototyped as an eight-to-twelve-second sequence before any full build or Three.js decision.

## Open decisions

- What sensation should the first five seconds create for TTA's founder: immediate recognition, surprise, desire, authority, or another priority?
- Which generic interior-design website convention must the concept most clearly reject?
- Which residential project or coherent asset family has the strongest visual continuity and enough resolution for the opening sequence?
- Which source assets can support portrait-native composition, full-width presentation, controlled parallax, or image-to-video treatment without misrepresentation?
- What single signature interaction could carry the concept while leaving enough stillness for the work to be read?
- Which founder wording and formal identity should be approved if TTA chooses to continue after seeing the prototype?
- Which services, process stages, project claims, inquiry route, and asset rights require post-interest discovery rather than inclusion now?
